# Global Renewable Energy Transition Dashboard (2015–2024)
## Project Overview
This project focuses on analyzing global renewable energy trends from 2015 to 2024 using Tableau.
The dashboard explores renewable energy production, capacity and investment across different countries and regions. It also compares renewable energy adoption among different income groups.
The main purpose of this project is to understand how renewable energy is developing around the world through data visualizations.

## Project Objectives
-Analyze global renewable energy production.
-Compare renewable energy performance across countries.
-Explore renewable energy capacity across different income levels.
-Examine investment patterns.
-Understand changes in renewable energy capacity from 2015 to 2024.

## Tools and Technologies

-Data Visualization
-Data Analysis

## Key Findings
Based on the dashboard analysis:
-India recorded the highest displayed renewable energy production, at approximately 3,690,246 GWh.
-South Korea followed with approximately 2,217,977 GWh of production.
-China and Chile also showed high renewable energy production, with approximately 1,326,886 GWh and 1,227,570 GWh respectively.
-High-income countries recorded the highest displayed renewable energy capacity, at 2,952,240 MW.
-Lower-middle-income countries followed with 2,062,248 MW of capacity.
-The transition trend shows an overall increase in renewable energy capacity across several regions between 2015 and 2024.
-The dashboard also shows some missing or unclassified records.

## Business Insights and Recommendations
-Countries with higher renewable energy production can be studied to understand the factors supporting their renewable energy development.
-The differences in renewable energy capacity across income groups highlight the importance of considering economic conditions when planning renewable energy development.
-Regional trends can help identify areas where renewable energy capacity is growing more slowly.
-Improving missing or unclassified data can support more accurate analysis.
-Investment and production patterns can be studied together to support better renewable energy planning and decision-making.
